# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors and leaders pledge to make participation in the Human Adaptive Technology Studio a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility, apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community
- Taking seriously the fact that our work involves research participants, and treating information about them with care

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
- Sharing information about research participants outside the boundaries of the relevant approval
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Notes for an interdisciplinary research setting

This Studio brings together people from different fields, at different career stages, with different assumptions about what is normal. A few things follow from that:

- **Disciplines disagree about what counts as rigour.** Treat a methodological disagreement as a disagreement, not as evidence that someone does not know what they are doing.
- **Power differences are real.** Students, junior researchers and interns are less able to push back on a senior colleague. If you are senior, invite disagreement explicitly and notice when you are not getting any.
- **Not knowing something is not a failing.** Nobody here is expert across sensing, modelling, XR, health and governance. Questions are how interdisciplinary work happens.
- **Credit matters.** Acknowledge contributions accurately, including those of students and research assistants.
- **Care for participants extends beyond ethics forms.** How we talk about the people in our studies, in meetings and in code comments, is part of this.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Studio leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful.

Studio leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all Studio spaces — repositories, issues, pull requests and reviews — and also applies when an individual is officially representing the Studio in public spaces.

## Enforcement guidelines

Studio leaders will follow these guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct.

**1. Correction.** *Impact:* Use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed unprofessional or unwelcome. *Consequence:* A private, written warning, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

**2. Warning.** *Impact:* A violation through a single incident or series of actions. *Consequence:* A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the people involved for a specified period. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

**3. Temporary ban.** *Impact:* A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour. *Consequence:* A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period.

**4. Permanent ban.** *Impact:* Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals. *Consequence:* A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

These guidelines describe how the Studio manages its own spaces. They are not disciplinary procedures, and they operate independently of any institutional process that may also apply.
